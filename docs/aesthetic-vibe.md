# Morulium — Aesthetic & Vibe

The felt sense of the game's visual identity — the north star every asset designs *toward*.
This is not a UI spec: screen layout stays deferred until the core loop settles. Use this to keep
the wordmark, favicon, sprites, and one day the UI all speaking the same language.

---

## The one-line

**Clinical dark-biotech villainy.** You are a geneticist-warlord growing monsters in vats to
conquer the world — a sinister, *competent* laboratory rendered in elegant menace, not gore.
The name says it: *Morulium*, the place where new life is grown.

---

## The two registers

Everything lives on one of two axes. Keep them distinct so the player always knows which half of
the game they're standing in — and the tension between them *is* the brand: a lab that grows
weapons of flesh, and a war machine that runs on them.

- **Cultivation — the lab.** Clinical biotech. Glass and fluid, vats and membranes,
  bioluminescence, brass and riveted iron, gauges and tubing. Cold precision with something alive
  and wet inside it. This is the Vivarium, the Vat, the DNA Lab, the Registry, Harvesting and
  breeding — most of the game's "home" surfaces.
- **Conquest — the map.** Imperial-military. Stencilled metal, stamped authority, territory and
  occupation, rust and iron, fronts and banners. Colder, harder, less organic. This is Incursions,
  Occupations, the region map.

---

## Tone

Elegant-sinister, **not uber-gross.** The horror is biotech-uncanny — veined, translucent, glowing,
wet — not splatter or viscera. Clinical grotesque: the unease comes from cold competence and
things-grown-that-shouldn't-be, not from gore on screen. The villainy is confident and controlled —
a quiet menace, not cartoon evil. The lab is immaculate; the thing in the tank is watching you.

## Palette

| Role | Color |
|---|---|
| Ground | near-black teal-blacks (most surfaces sit dark) |
| Structure | deep teal |
| Depth / shadow / menace | bruised purple |
| **Hero accent** | sickly bio-green glow — bioluminescence, glowing fluid, living cells |
| Machinery / military | rust, iron, steel |

Usage: dark grounds throughout; **bio-green is the single hero accent** — glow, life, active
elements — used *sparingly* so it reads as genuinely alive. Rust and iron carry the machinery and
the military register; bruised purple carries depth and dread. Register lean: lab surfaces skew
teal/green/glass, military surfaces skew rust/iron/darker.

**Discipline:** curated ramps only, never raw color — the same rule that keeps the procedural
sprites from looking like garbage. High contrast, especially at small sizes.

---

## Materials & motifs

Vats and tanks; viscous amber-green fluid; suspended embryos and morula cell-clusters; membranes,
veins, translucent flesh; feed-tubes, cabling, brass valves, riveted iron; condensation on glass;
gauges and dials; glow rising from within. On the military side: stencils, stamps, scratched and
rusted plate, territory maps.

The signature motif, worth repeating across assets: **organic growth overtaking metal** — flesh and
veins creeping across industrial surfaces, exactly the wordmark treatment. It's the whole premise in
one image: life engineered out of a cold machine.

---

## Typography

- **Display / branding:** heavy, condensed, stencil-ish — industrial/imperial letterforms,
  optionally corrupted by biotech growth (the wordmark: riveted iron overtaken by glowing veins and
  slow drips).
- **UI / data:** clean, legible, clinical — a lab readout. Function over flourish; data screens
  should feel like operating instruments.

The split mirrors the registers: stamped-metal headers, clinical body text.

---

## Pixel art

The house medium — PixelLab for static/branding assets, code-drawn procedural sprites for units.
Flat-stylized, strong silhouettes, chunky readable shapes, dramatic rim lighting, high contrast,
restrained detail, curated palette always. The favicon is a glowing morula cell-cluster. The unit
sprites are **readable-from-genome** (a mechanic wearing an art costume, not decoration) and must
look intentional, never programmer-art.

---

## The feel of the UI (aspirational — not a layout)

When the UI pass eventually comes, it should feel like operating a villain's specimen-management
console: dark, instrument-like, data-dense but legible, glowing accents on a dead-black ground, the
sense of machinery under your hands. The information-hiding ethos shows up visually too — the game
*withholds* (a sparse Registry, qualitative readouts instead of raw numbers), which should read as
clinical opacity, not clutter. Menacing calm, not busy.

---

## What it's NOT (guardrails)

- **Not** gore/splatter horror — biotech-uncanny, not viscera.
- **Not** cartoonish or campy evil — competent, cold menace.
- **Not** clean chrome sci-fi — it's grimy, wet, industrial biotech.
- **Not** a cute monster-collector — sinister, not adorable.
- **Not** the WaifuHatch look — we share mechanics, not aesthetics: nothing sexual, nothing
  bright-gacha-candy.

---

## Touchstones (the neighborhood, not the target)

The villain-lab management energy of *Evil Genius*; the organic-industrial dread of Giger-inflected
biotech horror; the sickly-bioluminescent clinical decay of dark sci-fi labs. Borrow the mood, not
the assets.

---

## Asset do / don't

**Do:** dark grounds · one glowing accent · silhouette-first · curated palette · high contrast ·
elegant menace · organic-overtaking-metal.

**Don't:** raw or random color · gratuitous gore · bright saturated candy palettes · busy
low-contrast detail (dies at small sizes) · cute or comedic framing.
